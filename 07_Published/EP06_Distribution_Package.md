### Newsletter Lead Section
The CapEx Trap: Why Owning the Intelligence Stack Threatens Q3 Margins

Amazon’s recent decision to wind down most of its flagship AI models marks a decisive shift in enterprise tech strategy. When a tech giant with virtually unlimited infrastructure retreats from foundational model development due to unsustainable compute and CapEx overhead, a critical question emerges for enterprise leaders: Are you over-capitalizing internal decision systems by trying to own the intelligence stack rather than architecting model-agnostic operational workflows that protect long-term margins?

For the past eighteen months, corporate boardrooms rushed to build proprietary models, mistaking infrastructure ownership for a competitive moat. However, the marginal ROI of training bespoke foundational models has hit a wall of diminishing returns. As compute costs compound and model deprecation accelerates, bespoke decision systems are rapidly morphing from strategic assets into margin-draining liabilities.

In the coming quarter, operational net margins will diverge sharply along architectural lines. Companies locked into heavy, single-model CapEx commitments will see operating expenses swell without proportional efficiency gains. Conversely, resilient enterprises are pivoting toward model-agnostic operational workflows. By decoupling decision logic from underlying model providers, forward-looking CFOs and CTOs can dynamically route tasks to the most cost-effective intelligence API, treating compute as a variable utility rather than a capital sink.

The imperative for Q3 is clear: stop trying to build the engine when you only need to steer the car. Architecting flexible, model-agnostic decision workflows protects long-term margins, mitigates vendor lock-in, and transforms AI from a margin tax into a sustainable productivity multiplier.
